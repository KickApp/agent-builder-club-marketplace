# Reading a meeting note

A note or transcript becomes three lines on the client card, written in plain words with the
month of the note in parentheses. Notes never supply fees, hours, or revenue figures; a
remark like "we did about two million" is a revenue band on the Business line and nothing
more.

## What counts as a note

Anything with dialogue, a meeting structure, action items, or dated observations about a
client. Read files by content, not filename. Match the client by the roster's rules; a note
that names no listed client goes on the unmatched list with the names it mentions. When a
notes tool responds, search it by the client's name and aliases for the period and treat what
it returns like file text.

## The Business line

What the business does, its revenue band (under $250k, $250k to $1M, $1M to $5M, over $5M),
entity type and count, the tools they run on (ledger, payroll, payments), location. One clear
cue per fact; two cues that conflict mean "unclear" with both paraphrased.

## The "Working with them" line

How the relationship actually runs, in a clause or two:

- Documents: in before you ask, in when asked, or chased.
- Decisions: who makes them (owner, controller, CFO, a board) and how fast (on the call,
  within a week, a month or more).
- Organization: personal spending mixed into the business account, receipts in a shoebox,
  books current or behind.
- Temperament: pushes back on fees or scope, or accepts them; answers once or asks the same
  question again.

## The Signals line

Good and bad, in words, each with the month it appeared:

- Good: referred someone (a specific person or firm asked who does their books because of
  this client; an intention to refer does not count), asked to buy more (forecasting,
  payroll, advisory, a second entity), praised the work, paid promptly when the note says so.
- Bad: paid late or needed reminders, disputed an invoice or the scope, work done outside
  scope without a fee, statements or documents chased, the same question answered again,
  catch-up or cleanup work, months behind.

Silence is not a signal. A client whose notes never mention payment is not "pays on time";
the line just says nothing about payment.

## Books condition cues from notes

Feed the card's Books condition line: statements missing or chased, receipts in bulk,
personal and business spending mixed, months of catch-up at onboarding or later, corrections
made more than once, a booking or payment platform never reconciled. Two or more of these
reads messy; so does either severe item alone (personal spending mixed into the business
account, or a catch-up of three months or more). One ordinary item reads fair. Same rule as
the client card.

## Quotes

At most two short quotes per client, each under 25 words, each cited as file and line (or
the tool's item), each attached to the fact it supports. Paraphrase everything else with a
citation. Leave out private detail that does not bear on the engagement: health, family,
legal matters.
