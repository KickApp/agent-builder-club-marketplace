# Options

## Purpose

Turn the profile into five or six acquisition moves, each pointing at a named client or a
stated trait, each scored in a word, ending with a question for the partners rather than a
plan. This is the third output, on request.

1. Referral asks to the named best clients, first: cheapest, and on-profile by
   construction.
2. Places the profile already gathers, and one message.
3. One packaging or pricing change for the biggest driver in the ranking.
4. A double-down move when the top group shares a source.
5. The two-moves question and the partner-review line.

## Procedure

- [ ] 1. Take the profile section, the ranked table, and the `Firm` note from the
      conversation.
      The `Firm` note gives where the last five clients came from, capacity, and the
      services to grow or exit. No profile: ask for the profile step by name. This skill
      does not build one.
- [ ] 2. Write the referral asks.
      For each top-group client, one move naming the specific introduction the card
      supports (the plumbing company Bob mentioned, the two Philadelphia agencies Jim
      named, the investor's portfolio). No card support: one general referral ask for the
      whole group.
- [ ] 3. Name where the profile gathers, one or two moves, as channel families.
      From the fields that passed: the associations, suppliers, and events of the industry,
      if an industry passed; the advisors that businesses of that size and entity type
      already pay (lenders, attorneys, insurance brokers, franchisors); the user
      communities of the tools they run on.
- [ ] 4. Write the message.
      One line the firm can say to a prospect who fits the profile, built from what the
      top group asked for and how they like to work ("a monthly close you can hand a
      lender without rework").
- [ ] 5. Add one packaging or pricing move for the biggest driver in the ranking.
      A scope line in the engagement letter for unbilled work; an onboarding cleanup fee or
      a minimum standard for messy books; a fee floor or an 18-month fee review for stale
      fees; a packaged offer for the service the top group keeps asking for. Name the
      clients it came from.
- [ ] 6. Read the "Came to you" line on the top group's cards.
      Two or more arrived the same way: one move to make that source routine (the referral
      ask at every quarterly review, the event booked again). Otherwise, or when the lines
      read unknown, skip the move and say why in one line.
- [ ] 7. Score each move and cap the list.
      One word each for effort, impact, and time to first client (weeks, months,
      quarters), with one clause of reasoning. Sequence by capacity when the owner gave
      one. Cap at six; drop anything without a client or trait behind it.
- [ ] 8. Close and stop.
      Two lines: "If you could do two of these before <date>, which two, and who owns
      them?", with the date 30 days from today, then "Review with your partners before
      acting." Hand off: "That is the engagement; one question before we finish."

## Caveats

- No invented market statistics: no market size, conversion rate, or benchmark. An
  owner-supplied figure is quoted as theirs.
- No recommendation to buy a tool or hire a vendor. Naming a tool the top group already
  runs on, as the place its users gather, is a channel and is fine.
- No authoritative pricing or marketing advice. Every move is an option for the partners.
- Client names appear in the firm-internal version only.
- Never ask mid-run. Questions belong in the closing lines.
- Thin inputs change the section without stopping it:
  - No capacity: moves without sequencing, and a line saying capacity would order them.
  - No client sources from the interview: skip the double-down move.
  - A hypothesis-grade profile: the "Rests on" line says the moves rest on a hypothesis
    from n clients.
  - No notes: the referral asks are general, not specific.
- An owner's correction (channels the firm will never use, a preferred number of moves)
  applies at once and can be kept in the `Firm notes` block. It never adds market
  statistics or vendor names.

## Example

Run on 2026-09-14, so the closing question's date is 30 days later.

```markdown
## Where to find more of them
Rests on: established Scranton businesses with $1M to $5M in revenue, clean books, documents before every call, asking for forecasts; a hypothesis from 2 clients. Capacity: 6 new clients this year (yours).

| # | Move | Rests on | Effort | Impact | First client in | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ask Bob Vance for the introduction to the plumbing company he mentioned in March | Vance card, Mar 2026 note | low | high | weeks | a warm name already exists |
| 2 | Ask Pinecrest's office manager for a referral at the next quarterly review | Pinecrest card, no named introduction | low | medium | months | on-profile by construction, no name yet |
| 3 | Meet the Scranton lenders who finance businesses of this size | Business size field; Pinecrest came through a lender | medium | medium | months | lenders already see these clients' statements |
| 4 | Say it the same way every time: "a monthly close and a quarterly forecast you can hand your lender without rework" | what Pinecrest and Vance asked for | low | medium | weeks | built from the top group's own requests |
| 5 | Add an onboarding cleanup fee and a minimum books standard to the engagement letter | Larkin card (messy books, fee not raised since 2023) | low | medium | weeks | stops the next Larkin before it starts |
| 6 | Show up where Gusto users in the region meet | Tools field (both on Gusto) | medium | low | quarters | a channel the top group already uses |

No double-down move: Pinecrest came through a lender and Vance through a Chamber of Commerce contact, so no single source repeats.

If you could do two of these before 2026-10-14, which two, and who owns them?
Review with your partners before acting.

That is the engagement; one question before we finish.
```

## Completion

Done when:
- Five or six moves, each naming the client or trait it rests on.
- Referral asks to named clients appear first.
- Every score has one clause of reasoning.
- No market size, conversion rate, benchmark, or vendor recommendation appears.
- The section ends with the two-moves question dated 30 days out, the partner-review
  line, and the hand-off line.

Cleanup: the section stays in the conversation. The two-moves question is the open item;
its answer feeds the orchestrator's close.
