# Options

## Summary

The third output, on request. Referral asks to the named best clients first, because they
are the cheapest and on-profile by construction. Then the places the profile already
gathers, one message, and one or two changes to packaging or pricing that fix the drivers
the ranking exposed. Six moves at most, each pointing at a client or a trait by name, each
scored in a word, ending with a question rather than a plan.

## Inputs

The profile section and the ranked table from the conversation, and the `Firm` note (where
the last five clients came from, capacity, services to grow or exit). Without a profile,
ask for the profile step by name; this skill does not build one.

## Workflow

1. Referral asks. For each client in the top group, one move naming the specific
   introduction the card supports (the plumbing company Bob mentioned, the two Philadelphia
   agencies Jim named, the investor's portfolio). No card support means a general referral
   ask to that client, one move for the whole group.
2. Where the profile gathers. From the fields that passed: the associations, suppliers, and
   events of the industry if one passed; the advisors that businesses of that size and
   entity type already pay (lenders, attorneys, insurance brokers, franchisors); the user
   communities of the tools they run on. One or two moves, named as channel families.
3. The message. One line the firm can say to a prospect who fits the profile, built from
   what the top group asked for and how they like to work ("a monthly close you can hand a
   lender without rework").
4. Packaging and pricing. One move for the biggest driver in the ranking: a scope line in
   the engagement letter for unbilled work; an onboarding cleanup fee or a minimum standard
   for messy books; a fee floor or an 18-month fee review for stale fees; a packaged offer
   for the service the top group keeps asking for. Name the clients it came from.
5. Double down. Read the "Came to you" line on the top group's cards. If two or more
   arrived the same way, one move to make that source routine (the referral ask at every
   quarterly review, the event booked again). If the lines read unknown, skip this step and
   say so in one line.
6. Score each move in a word for effort, impact, and time to first client (weeks, months,
   quarters) with one clause of reasoning. Sequence by capacity when the owner gave one.
   Cap at six; drop anything without a client or trait behind it.
7. Deliver and stop. Hand off: "That is the engagement; one question before we finish."

## Degradation

| Missing | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Capacity | Moves without sequencing; say that capacity would order them |
| Client sources from the interview | Skip the double-down move |
| A hypothesis-grade profile | Header says the moves rest on a hypothesis from <n> clients |
| No notes | Referral asks are general, not specific |

## When to ask vs proceed

Never mid-run. Questions belong in the closing line.

## Completion criteria

- [ ] Five or six moves, each naming the client or trait it rests on
- [ ] Referral asks to named clients appear first
- [ ] Every score has one clause of reasoning
- [ ] No market size, conversion rate, benchmark, or vendor recommendation
- [ ] Ends with the first-30-days question and the partner-review line

## Guardrails

- No invented market statistics; an owner-supplied figure is quoted as theirs.
- No recommendation to buy a tool or hire a vendor. Naming a tool the top group already
  runs on, as the place its users gather, is a channel and is fine.
- No authoritative pricing or marketing advice; every move is an option for the partners.
- Client names in the firm-internal version only.

## Deliverable

Voice, for every document this skill writes: a careful accountant's prose, not an
assistant's. Short declarative sentences with varied length, sentence-case headings,
concrete nouns and real figures. Banned tells: em dashes; filler words (delve, robust,
seamless, leverage, comprehensive, crucial); the "not just X, but Y" construction; AI
boilerplate ("It's important to note", "In conclusion", "I hope this helps"); decorative
emoji; bolded keyword openers in prose. Read a sentence back; if no accountant would say
it aloud to a client, rewrite it.

```markdown
## Where to find more of them
Rests on: <profile sentence>; <hypothesis note if any>. Capacity: <n> new clients this year (yours).

| # | Move | Rests on | Effort | Impact | First client in | Why |
| 1 | Ask Bob Vance for the introduction to the plumbing company he mentioned in March | Vance card, Mar 2026 note | low | high | weeks | a warm name already exists |
| ... |

If you could do two of these before <date>, which two, and who owns them?
Review with your partners before acting.
```

## Learned preferences

Apply an owner's correction immediately and keep it for the rest of the run. When a
correction should stick (channels the firm will never use, a preferred number of moves),
offer to save it as a dated entry under this heading with the owner's approval. Preferences
never add market statistics or vendor names. If this file is not writable here, hand the
owner the text.
