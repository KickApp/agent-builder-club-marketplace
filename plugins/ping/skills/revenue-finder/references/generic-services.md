# Generic service catalog

Any run that uses this file must label its results "generic catalog".

A starter list for firms that have not supplied their own. Descriptions matter more than names,
because matching reads the client's sentence against the description.

**Replace this.** Every run against this file is labeled `generic catalog`, and it will surface
services the firm may not sell. Swapping in a real catalog is the biggest single improvement
available to this skill.

No prices here on purpose. A made-up price is worse than no price. Add real ones or let the
report rank on quote strength alone.

---

**Bookkeeping**
Recording and reconciling transactions, bank and card reconciliation, maintaining clean books
month to month. Signals: books are behind, the client is doing it themselves, a bookkeeper left,
records are a mess before tax time.

**Payroll**
Running payroll, calculating and filing payroll taxes, direct deposit, quarterly 940 and 941
filings, W-2 preparation. Signals: first hire, growing headcount, switching payroll providers,
frustration with a current provider, contractors becoming employees.

**Accounts payable and receivable**
Processing vendor bills, customer invoicing, collections, keeping both sides of cash healthy.
Signals: late-paying customers, bills getting missed, no invoicing process, cash tied up in
receivables.

**Cash flow forecasting**
Projecting cash in and out, working capital planning, spotting shortfalls before they arrive.
Signals: not knowing what cash looks like in 30 or 60 days, seasonal swings, a large purchase
being considered, worry about making payroll.

**Controller services**
Owning the accounting function, month-end close, management reporting, budget versus actual.
Signals: outgrowing basic bookkeeping, needing reporting for a lender or board, no one internally
owning the numbers.

**CFO and advisory**
Strategic planning, KPI and margin review, pricing decisions, growth and financing guidance.
Signals: expansion plans, opening a location, raising money, a big decision with no financial
analysis behind it, wanting to know which parts of the business make money.

**Budgeting and forecasting**
Annual budgets, rolling forecasts, scenario planning against the plan. Signals: no budget exists,
board or bank asking for projections, planning a hire or a major investment.

**Business tax preparation**
Preparing and filing business returns, coordinating with personal returns. Signals: new entity,
first year filing, an extension that keeps getting extended, a return prepared elsewhere going
wrong.

**Tax planning and strategy**
Year-round planning to reduce liability, entity structure, timing of income and expenses,
retirement and depreciation strategy. Signals: a surprise tax bill, asking whether to become an
S-Corp, a profitable year, a large asset purchase, an owner taking distributions with no plan.

**Multi-state and sales tax**
Nexus studies, registrations, sales tax filing across jurisdictions. Signals: selling into new
states, an e-commerce or marketplace channel, remote employees in other states, a nexus letter
from a state.

**Entity formation and new business setup**
Choosing a structure, EIN, opening books, chart of accounts, first-year planning. Signals:
starting something new, a side business becoming real, adding a second entity, a partner joining.

**R&D tax credit**
Identifying qualifying development work and claiming federal and state credits. Signals: building
software or products, engineering payroll, process development, technical problem solving.

**Audit and assurance**
Audits, reviews, and compilations for lenders, investors, boards, or grantors. Signals: a lender
or investor asking for audited statements, grant requirements, a board wanting assurance.

**Systems and software implementation**
Selecting and setting up accounting software, migrations, integrations, training. Signals:
outgrowing a spreadsheet, disconnected tools, manual data entry, a software change being
discussed.
